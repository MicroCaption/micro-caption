## 1. Session Goals

Session 9 shipped speaker diarization; on first live test it **didn't show any
speaker differentiation**. The ask was blunt and correct: it must *always*
properly show a change of speaker, on any source. This session is the debugging
story — reproducing the failure on real multi-speaker audio, measuring the
embeddings instead of guessing, and fixing three distinct defects.

Commits: `64c17e1` (coverage), `c3a083c` (separation + unified marks).

---

## 2. Reproducing the Failure (stop guessing)

The first live test was a single-narrator YouTube video, so "all SPEAKER 1, no
`>>`" was *arguably* correct — which made it tempting to wave away. Instead we
ran a **4-speaker presidential debate** (CNN simulcast) through the live pipeline
and inspected the stored cues:

- 149 cues, **all SPEAKER 1**, **zero speaker_change marks**.

So it was genuinely broken, not a single-speaker artifact. Two separate bugs.

---

## 3. Bug 1 — Coverage: continuous speech went unlabelled

On the *first* test stream (24 min) only ~325 / 1807 cues were labelled, in large
`None` blocks. Cause: `DiarizationPass` closed an utterance only on a pause >
`utterance_gap_seconds` (or end-of-stream). In continuous narration with few long
pauses, a "utterance" grew to **60 s+** — past the 65 s audio-retain buffer — so
by the time it was ready to embed, its audio had been **pruned and the whole
block skipped**.

**Fix (`64c17e1`):** cap a span at `max_utterance_seconds` (10 s) so continuous
speech is chopped into bounded, embeddable utterances; raise retain to
`settle + max_utt + 60 s`; count + log pruned skips. Coverage on the same stream
went ~18 % → ~99 %.

---

## 4. Bug 2 — No separation: the embeddings were collapsed (measure it)

Even with full coverage, the debate clustered to one speaker. Rather than tune
thresholds blind, we **captured 90 s of the debate via the project's own
GStreamer adapter** (no ffmpeg needed) and measured pairwise cosine over 3 s
windows with the real model:

| | min | p10 | median | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Raw embeddings** | 0.41 | 0.61 | **0.71** | 0.79 |

Different speakers at **0.71 cosine** — a working speaker space puts them near
0.0–0.3. The raw WeSpeaker vectors carry a large shared component (recording
channel / room / codec) that dominates cosine, so **no threshold can separate
them** (at 0.45 everything merges; even 0.70 merges).

**The standard fix is mean-normalisation.** Subtracting the running mean and
re-normalising:

| | min | p10 | median | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Centered** | −0.54 | −0.24 | **−0.04** | 0.24 |

Now there's real structure. Running the *implemented* clusterer on the centered
space recovered actual speaker turns — clean blocks like
`0000000…6666666…7777777` — instead of a flat `000000…`.

**Fix (`c3a083c`):**
- `OnlineSpeakerClusterer` keeps a running mean of all embeddings, stores
  centroids in **raw** space, and compares in **centered** space at assignment
  time (so centroids don't go stale as the mean settles). A short raw-merge
  warmup covers the cold start.
- The live `SpeakerChangeDetector` applies the same centering (stores the raw
  previous embedding, centers both with the current mean at comparison).
- Thresholds are now cosine similarities in the **centered** space (~0.10),
  documented in `settings.yaml`. Old raw-distance/similarity values were
  meaningless against the 0.71 floor.

---

## 5. Bug 3 — Marks and labels could disagree

The live `>>` used a *separate* fragile pairwise threshold from the clustering
that produced `SPEAKER N`, so they could contradict each other. Now the
behind-live pass **derives the `>>` mark from cluster-label transitions** (a new
label vs the previous utterance = a turn), writes it onto the stored cue + the
`speaker` SSE event, and the replay/Logs view renders it. The mark and the label
can no longer disagree.

## 8. Open Items / Tuning

- **Over-splitting during warmup.** Online greedy clustering over the unstable
  early mean spawns extra short-lived speakers (~8 for the debate's ~4 voices in
  90 s) before settling. Tunable via `center_warmup` / `cluster_threshold`; a
  periodic re-cluster over a rolling window would be the next robustness step.
- **Thresholds are starting points.** Defaults were validated on broadcast debate
  audio; council-meeting rooms differ. The capture harness can be pointed at a
  real meeting URL to measure and tune `cluster_threshold` and
  `change_threshold` on representative audio.
- **Tuning direction is a product call.** Council meetings = a frequent chair +
  many short public-comment speakers. Favour *over-split* (never miss a change)
  vs *merge* (clean, stable labels)? That sets which way the threshold goes.
- **Live surfacing of labels.** Live shows only the best-effort `>>`; the stable
  `SPEAKER N` labels are in replay/Logs. Surfacing the behind-live label into the
  live roll-up (with its few-second delay) is a deliberate next step if wanted.
- **ffmpeg absent.** `yt-dlp --download-sections` needs ffmpeg (not installed);
  the GStreamer adapter was used for offline capture instead. Fine, just noted.
